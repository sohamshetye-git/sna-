First-Step Markov Recurrence Formulation:
HT(y, y) = 0 HT(x, y) = 1 + ∑z ∈ N(x) P(x, z) · HT(z, y)   (∀ x ≠ y)

From node x, any random surfer expends 1 step to reach an adjacent neighbor z with probability P(x,z) = 1 / deg(x), and subsequently expects HT(z,y) additional steps to hit target y.

Asymmetry of Hitting Time

Unlike undirected shortest path distance, Hitting Time is asymmetric: $HT(x, y) \ne HT(y, x)$ in general. Hitting a peripheral node from a dense hub is often much slower than hitting the hub from the periphery!

Global vs. Local Heuristic

Common Neighbors and Jaccard only consider 2-hop local topologies. Hitting Time is a Global Heuristic that explores all possible infinite paths, capturing the global flow and bottlenecks of the entire network.

Why Normalized Hitting Time?

High-degree hubs (like Node B) have a high stationary distribution $\pi_B$, making them naturally easier to hit by chance. Multiplying by $\pi_y$ balances this bias so rare targets aren't unjustly penalized.
